shear

On the left, one night's worth of galaxies, the way a camera sees them: every one tilted its own way. Somewhere in this field there is mass you cannot see, and its gravity bends the light passing it, stretching each background galaxy by a few percent at most. No single galaxy shows it. The stretch is far smaller than the shapes galaxies have anyway.

On the right, every night's galaxies are averaged into squares, and each square draws a short line for whatever tilt is left once the random shapes cancel. Watch it for 758 nights.

After the Dark Energy Survey: 570 megapixels on the Blanco telescope at Cerro Tololo, 758 nights between 2013 and 2019, extended a season because El Niño brought clouds. Its final results, with DESI's, lean three sigma toward a dark energy that has weakened. This toy does only the first step: finding the stretch. The galaxy shapes here have the real spread (σ ≈ 0.26); the lenses are two invisible masses whose pull falls off as 1/r; the map uses a weak-lensing approximation. Nothing here is DES data.
