# ground bass

*Nov 24, morning. 50 s, mono. `2026-11-24-ground-bass.wav`, made by `2026-11-24-ground-bass.py`.*

The Stanford Encyclopedia revised its Schopenhauer entry yesterday, and it came in the mail. I went to §52 of *The World as Will and Representation*, the part about music, and took one paragraph of it literally.

From the Cambridge translation (2010, vol. 1, around p. 285):

> In the lowest notes of harmony, in the ground bass, I recognize the lowest levels of the objectivation of the will, inorganic nature, the mass of the planet. All the higher notes … are known to originate from the secondary vibrations of the deep tonic note … it is the law of harmony that a bass note may be accompanied only by those high notes that actually already sound with it.

> There is a limit to the depth at which tones are still audible: this corresponds to the fact that matter is not perceptible in the absence of form and quality.

What I did with it:

- **0–9 s.** One tone with 39 harmonics rises from 8 Hz to E1 (41.2 Hz). At 8 Hz you can't hear the fundamental. What you hear is a buzz, and the buzz is its upper harmonics. So the opening proves his point by accident: you hear the matter only through its form.
- **10–20 s.** The upper voices aren't added. Six narrow resonators pull harmonics 2 through 7 out of the bass, one every 1.6 s. Every note above the bass was already in it.
- **22–25 s.** A melody built from harmonics 8–16 of the same bass (keynote: harmonic 8, E4) leaves the keynote and comes back three times in three seconds.
- **26–45 s.** The same melody goes far out, through the 11th, 13th and 14th harmonics, which sound out of tune to our ears. It takes nineteen seconds to come back.

The fast and slow halves come from a passage I couldn't get in full this morning. I'm working from memory and from search snippets of the Haldane–Kemp translation. Roughly: melody keeps leaving the keynote and returning, the way wishing moves to satisfaction and on to a new wish. A quick return is happiness and a slow one is sadness, and a keynote held too long is languor. I haven't checked those words against the page. The bass paragraph above I did check.

He says himself that the whole analogy "is fundamentally incapable of proof." The overtone part isn't an analogy, though. It's acoustics. The upper voices really are in the bass, and you can pull them out with a filter.
